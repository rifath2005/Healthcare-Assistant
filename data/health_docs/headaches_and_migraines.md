# Clinical Guidelines: Tension Headaches vs. Migraines

## Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) & MedlinePlus
**URL:** https://www.cdc.gov/headaches/index.html

### 1. Tension Headaches
Tension-type headaches are the most frequent headache disorder among adults. They typically manifest as a continuous, dull, aching pressure distributed bilaterally across the forehead, temples, or base of the neck. Unlike migraines, tension headaches are generally not exacerbated by mild physical activity and lack nausea or persistent visual aura.

**Primary Triggers:**
- Prolonged psychological stress or muscular strain from screen posture
- Sleep deprivation or irregular circadian sleep cycles
- Inadequate hydration and skipped meals

**Evidence-Based Management:**
- Hydration therapy (500ml-1000ml water intake)
- Gentle neck stretches and ergonomic adjustments
- Over-the-counter analgesics (acetaminophen, ibuprofen) under recommended package limits

### 2. Migraine Headaches
Migraines are neurovascular events characterized by recurring, moderate-to-severe throbbing or pulsating pain, often unilateral. Between 20% to 30% of sufferers experience an antecedent visual aura (flashing lights, zigzag patterns, or blind spots) 20 to 60 minutes before headache onset.

**Hallmark Symptoms:**
- Sensory hypersensitivity: Photophobia (light sensitivity) and phonophobia (sound sensitivity)
- Gastrointestinal involvement: Nausea and vomiting
- Aggravation by routine physical exertion

### 3. Red-Flag Warning Signs (Requires Immediate Emergency Evaluation)
Immediate emergency clinical care (call 112 / 108 or visit emergency department) is required if any of the following "SNOOP" criteria occur:
- "Thunderclap" headache: Instantaneous maximum intensity within seconds
- Neurological deficits: Sudden numbness, facial drooping, slurred speech, or limb weakness
- Systemic symptoms: High fever, stiff neck, confusion, or altered mental status
