# Behavioral Wellness: Managing Acute Anxiety, Stress & Grounding Protocols

## Source: National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) & MedlinePlus
**URL:** https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders

### 1. Neurobiology of Acute Stress & Panic
When an individual encounters an intense perceived stressor, the sympathetic autonomic nervous system triggers the "fight-or-flight" physiological response. Adrenaline and cortisol surge into the bloodstream, manifesting in:
- Tachycardia (elevated heart rate) and palpitations
- Hyperventilation (rapid, shallow chest breathing)
- Diaphoresis (cold sweats) and trembling
- Cognitive derealization or acute sense of impending doom

### 2. Evidence-Based Grounding Interventions

#### Technique A: 4-7-8 Diaphragmatic Breathing
Developed by Dr. Andrew Weil, the 4-7-8 breathing pattern serves as a natural tranquilizer for the nervous system:
1. Inhale quietly through the nose for 4 seconds.
2. Hold the breath smoothly for 7 seconds.
3. Exhale completely and audibly through the mouth for 8 seconds.
Repeating this cycle 4 times stimulates the vagus nerve and activates the parasympathetic relaxation response.

#### Technique B: Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)
Used by tactical responders and clinical athletes:
1. Inhale for 4 seconds.
2. Hold for 4 seconds.
3. Exhale for 4 seconds.
4. Hold lungs empty for 4 seconds.

#### Technique C: 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding
This cognitive orientation technique counters dissociative panic by anchoring attention to present sensory stimuli:
- **5 things you can SEE:** Notice textures, colors, and distant shapes.
- **4 things you can TOUCH:** Feel your feet on the floor, texture of your clothing, or cool surface of a desk.
- **3 things you can HEAR:** Listen for distant traffic, humming air vents, or clock ticking.
- **2 things you can SMELL:** Scent of soap, coffee, or fresh air.
- **1 thing you can TASTE:** Sip of water, mint, or the natural taste on your tongue.
