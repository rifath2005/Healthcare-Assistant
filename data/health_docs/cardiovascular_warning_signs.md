# Cardiovascular Health & Emergency Warning Indicators

## Source: American Heart Association (AHA)
**URL:** https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/warning-signs-of-a-heart-attack

### 1. Recognizing Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack)
A heart attack occurs when the flow of blood that brings oxygen to a part of the heart muscle suddenly becomes blocked. Prompt medical intervention saves lives and limits irreversible damage.

**Key Warning Signs:**
- **Chest Discomfort:** Most heart attacks involve discomfort in the center or left side of the chest that lasts more than a few minutes or that goes away and comes back. It can feel like uncomfortable pressure, squeezing, fullness, or pain.
- **Upper Body Discomfort:** Pain or discomfort in one or both arms, the back, neck, jaw, or stomach.
- **Shortness of Breath:** With or without chest discomfort.
- **Atypical Presentations in Women:** Women are more likely to experience shortness of breath, unexplained nausea/vomiting, back pain, or extreme unexplained exhaustion rather than standard crushing chest pain.

### 2. Immediate Clinical Protocol
If you suspect someone is suffering from a heart attack:
1. Call 911 or emergency medical services immediately.
2. Do not attempt to drive yourself to the hospital.
3. If advised by the emergency dispatcher, chew and swallow an adult non-enteric coated aspirin (325mg) while awaiting emergency responders, provided there is no aspirin allergy or active gastrointestinal bleed.
